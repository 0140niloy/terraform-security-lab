PHASE 3 - REGULATORY AND COMPLIANCE ASSESSMENT
================================================

Status:
Synthetic incident-response exercise. This document identifies potentially
relevant regulatory areas for legal/compliance review. It does not constitute
legal advice or a definitive determination of reporting obligations.

1. GLBA / Safeguards Rule
-------------------------
Potential relevance:
Unauthorized access to customer financial information may trigger
financial-privacy and information-security obligations.

Review:
- Nature and scope of customer information involved
- Whether information was encrypted
- Whether encryption keys were accessible
- Number of affected consumers
- Applicable notification requirements
- Incident-response and service-provider obligations

2. SEC CYBERSECURITY DISCLOSURE REQUIREMENTS
---------------------------------------------
Potential relevance:
A public company should evaluate whether a cybersecurity incident is
material and whether applicable SEC disclosure requirements are triggered.

Review:
- Materiality determination
- Date materiality was determined
- Impact on operations and financial condition
- Form 8-K requirements, if applicable
- Periodic-report disclosures
- Board and management involvement

3. SEC REGULATION S-P
---------------------
Potential relevance:
If covered customer information or records were accessed or used without
authorization, determine whether Regulation S-P requirements apply.

Review:
- Whether the organization is a covered institution
- Nature of customer information involved
- Unauthorized access/use determination
- Customer notification requirements
- Service-provider notification obligations

4. FINRA
--------
Potential relevance:
If the organization or affected activities fall within FINRA's regulatory
scope, review applicable cybersecurity, recordkeeping, reporting, and
supervisory obligations.

Review:
- Entity's FINRA status
- Affected systems and regulated activities
- Required records
- Incident reporting obligations
- Supervisory and business-continuity requirements

5. GDPR
--------
Potential relevance:
If affected individuals or processing activities fall within GDPR scope,
evaluate applicable personal-data breach obligations.

Review:
- Territorial and material scope
- Categories of personal data
- Number of affected individuals
- Risk to individuals
- Supervisory-authority notification requirements
- Data-subject notification requirements

6. CCPA / CPRA
--------------
Potential relevance:
If California residents and applicable covered data/business criteria are
involved, evaluate applicable privacy and breach-notification obligations.

Review:
- Number and identity of potentially affected California residents
- Data categories involved
- Applicability thresholds
- Notification requirements
- Consumer-rights implications

7. CORPORATE / INSURANCE / LAW-ENFORCEMENT REVIEW
---------------------------------------------------
Review:
- Cyber-insurance notification requirements
- Preservation of policy-required evidence
- Outside counsel involvement
- Regulatory notification strategy
- Law-enforcement referral where appropriate
- Contractual notification obligations

IMPORTANT LIMITATIONS
---------------------
The simulated evidence does not establish:
- The identity of any real attacker
- That an actual customer breach occurred
- The exact number of affected individuals
- That data was actually exfiltrated
- That any particular regulatory threshold was met
- That a specific filing or notification is legally required

All final legal and regulatory determinations require review of the actual
facts by qualified legal and compliance personnel.
