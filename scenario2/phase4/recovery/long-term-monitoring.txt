SCENARIO 2 - PHASE 4
LONG-TERM RECOVERY AND ENHANCED MONITORING PLAN

STATUS: SIMULATION / DEFENSIVE DESIGN

OBJECTIVE

Establish a two-year recovery and monitoring program following a
significant privileged-access security incident.

YEAR 1 - IMMEDIATE RECOVERY

0-30 DAYS

- Complete forensic preservation.
- Validate account containment.
- Rotate affected credentials.
- Review privileged permissions.
- Verify audit logging.
- Validate endpoint integrity.
- Review database access.
- Confirm DLP enforcement.
- Assess patient impact.
- Complete required legal/privacy review.

30-90 DAYS

- Implement JIT/PAM.
- Enforce phishing-resistant MFA.
- Harden privileged workstations.
- Improve database access separation.
- Deploy enhanced UEBA.
- Review service accounts.
- Validate backup security.
- Review encryption-key separation.

90-180 DAYS

- Conduct privileged-access certification.
- Perform detection engineering validation.
- Conduct incident-response tabletop exercises.
- Test DLP controls.
- Review cloud identity controls.
- Perform controlled security assessments.

180-365 DAYS

- Conduct independent control assessment.
- Perform privileged-access red-team exercise.
- Review long-term detection metrics.
- Reassess risk and residual exposure.
- Update incident-response procedures.

YEAR 2 - SUSTAINED ASSURANCE

MONTHS 12-18

- Repeat privileged-access certification.
- Review identity and PAM architecture.
- Validate database audit protection.
- Test backup restoration.
- Review UEBA effectiveness.
- Review security exceptions.

MONTHS 18-24

- Conduct independent security assessment.
- Perform insider-threat tabletop exercise.
- Test patient-safety incident response.
- Review DLP and egress controls.
- Review encryption-key governance.
- Update architecture based on lessons learned.

2. PATIENT SUPPORT

Where legally and operationally appropriate:
- Patient notification
- Dedicated support channel
- Identity-theft/credit monitoring where appropriate
- Fraud-awareness information
- Additional support for affected populations

The specific support package should depend on the confirmed nature and
scope of the incident.

3. EXTERNAL EXPOSURE MONITORING

Organizations may establish lawful monitoring for:
- Exposed organizational credentials
- Confirmed leaked datasets
- Known organizational identifiers
- Threat intelligence indicators
- Relevant public disclosures

Monitoring must not involve unauthorized access to systems or accounts.

4. COMPETITOR / THREAT-ACTOR COMPARISON

If stolen data or intellectual property is identified, investigators may
compare confirmed indicators against known threat campaigns and publicly
documented activity.

Comparison should be evidence-based and should not automatically imply
that a competitor or named threat actor was responsible.

5. RED-TEAM VALIDATION

Conduct controlled defensive exercises covering:
- Privileged account compromise
- Session theft scenarios
- Excessive database privileges
- USB/DLP controls
- External egress controls
- Audit-log protection
- Patient-safety impact

All testing must be authorized and performed against designated test
systems.

6. METRICS

Track:
- Standing privileged accounts
- JIT adoption
- MFA coverage
- PAM coverage
- Unauthorized access attempts
- DLP blocks
- Mean time to detect
- Mean time to contain
- Audit-log coverage
- Privileged-access review completion
- Security exceptions

7. GOVERNANCE

Review quarterly with:
- Security
- Privacy
- Compliance
- IT
- Database administration
- Clinical safety
- HR where appropriate
- Legal counsel where appropriate

CONCLUSION

Long-term monitoring should reduce recurrence risk while maintaining
privacy, proportionality, evidence integrity, and patient safety.

This is a defensive recovery and assurance plan.
