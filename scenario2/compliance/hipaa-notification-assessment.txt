============================================================

SIMULATION ONLY

IMPORTANT:
This document is a training artifact, not legal advice.


1. REGULATORY FRAMEWORK
------------------------------------------------------------

The HIPAA Breach Notification Rule governs breaches of unsecured
protected health information.

For a confirmed breach affecting 500 or more individuals:

- Individual notice is required without unreasonable delay and no later
  than 60 days after discovery.
- Notice to HHS/OCR is required without unreasonable delay and no later
  than 60 days after discovery.
- Prominent-media notice is required when the breach affects more than
  500 residents of a State or jurisdiction.

For fewer than 500 affected individuals, HHS reporting follows the
separate annual-reporting mechanism.

The organization must determine whether the event legally constitutes
a breach before final notification decisions are made.

3. INFORMATION REQUIRED FOR OCR REPORT
------------------------------------------------------------

The organization should be prepared to document:

- discovery date;
- incident date or date range;
- nature of the incident;
- systems involved;
- types of PHI involved;
- number of affected individuals;
- whether PHI was acquired or viewed;
- whether data was actually exfiltrated;
- containment actions;
- mitigation measures;
- corrective actions;
- contact information for the organization.


4. OCR INVESTIGATION PREPARATION
------------------------------------------------------------

Preserve:

- forensic timeline;
- database audit records;
- Okta records;
- VPN records;
- EDR telemetry;
- DLP events;
- network flows;
- change-management records;
- security policies;
- risk assessments;
- workforce-access policies;
- incident-response procedures;
- training records;
- evidence chain of custody.


5. SECURITY RULE CONTROL REVIEW
------------------------------------------------------------

ACCESS CONTROL
--------------
Was the DBA authorized to access patient-level data?

AUDIT CONTROLS
--------------
Why could sensitive-schema audit logging be altered?

AUTHENTICATION
--------------
Why could session contexts change without a new simulated MFA event?

INFORMATION ACCESS MANAGEMENT
-----------------------------
Was patient-level access necessary for the DBA's job function?

INTEGRITY
---------
Were clinical records modified or deleted?

TRANSMISSION SECURITY
---------------------
Why was an external SFTP attempt possible before being blocked?

WORKFORCE SECURITY
------------------
Were privileged-user access rights appropriately limited and reviewed?

RISK ANALYSIS
-------------
Did the organization's existing risk analysis identify privileged DBA
and hybrid-cloud insider-threat scenarios?


6. CONTROL-GAP QUESTIONS
------------------------------------------------------------

1. Can DBAs directly view patient-level PHI?
2. Can DBAs modify database audit configuration?
3. Can privileged sessions bypass centralized monitoring?
4. Can administrative credentials be reused across devices?
5. Can privileged users connect from unmanaged devices?
6. Are removable-media controls enforced technically?
7. Are external destinations restricted?
8. Are database audit logs immutable?
9. Are privileged sessions recorded?
10. Are emergency-access procedures documented and monitored?


7. CORRECTIVE-ACTION DOCUMENTATION
------------------------------------------------------------

Potential corrective actions:

- privileged-access redesign;
- database least privilege;
- immutable audit logging;
- centralized session monitoring;
- stronger device trust;
- hardware-backed authentication;
- DLP improvements;
- removable-media restrictions;
- improved network egress controls;
- UEBA;
- periodic privileged-access review;
- insider-threat response procedures.


8. PENALTY ASSESSMENT
------------------------------------------------------------

Do NOT calculate a fixed civil-money-penalty amount from the assignment's
"$100-$1.5M per violation" statement.

HIPAA civil money penalties are subject to statutory categories,
circumstances, inflation adjustments, and enforcement rules.

A real assessment should use the current HHS/OCR penalty schedule and
the facts of the specific violation.
