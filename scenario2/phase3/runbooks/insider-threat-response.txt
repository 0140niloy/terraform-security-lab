SCENARIO 2 - PHASE 3
PRIVILEGED INSIDER-THREAT RESPONSE RUNBOOK

STATUS: SIMULATION / DEFENSIVE DESIGN

OBJECTIVE
Provide a controlled response process when suspicious privileged-user
activity is detected.

PHASE 1 - DETECT

Trigger examples:
- Bulk PHI access
- Audit-log modification
- Unauthorized export
- Unknown privileged device
- Geographic session inconsistency
- Unauthorized HR access
- DLP block
- Suspicious PAM session

PHASE 2 - TRIAGE

Immediately establish:
- User/account
- Device
- Active sessions
- Systems accessed
- Data involved
- Current transfer status
- Security-control changes
- Whether patient safety may be affected

Do not assume intent at this stage.

PHASE 3 - CONTAIN

Depending on evidence and organizational policy:
- Suspend or restrict privileged access.
- Terminate active PAM sessions.
- Revoke temporary credentials.
- Isolate a compromised endpoint.
- Block suspicious data-transfer paths.
- Protect audit infrastructure.

Containment actions must preserve evidence whenever possible.

PHASE 4 - PRESERVE

Preserve:
- Identity-provider logs
- PAM recordings
- Endpoint telemetry
- Memory where appropriate
- Disk images
- Database audit logs
- Database transaction logs
- DLP events
- VPN/proxy logs
- Network flow
- Cloud audit logs
- Relevant change-management records

Record hashes, acquisition details, timestamps, investigator identity,
and evidence location.

PHASE 5 - INVESTIGATE

Evaluate competing hypotheses:

H1 - Malicious insider
H2 - Compromised account
H3 - Compromised workstation
H4 - Operational anomaly

Use objective evidence to update each hypothesis.

PHASE 6 - PATIENT SAFETY

Separately evaluate:
- Whether patient records were modified.
- Whether clinical systems were affected.
- Whether availability was affected.
- Whether incorrect information could reach clinical workflows.
- Whether patient harm is plausible.

Confidentiality, integrity, availability, and patient safety must be
assessed separately.

PHASE 7 - LEGAL / PRIVACY ESCALATION

Coordinate with:
- Incident response
- Privacy officer
- Compliance
- Legal counsel
- HR where appropriate
- Executive leadership
- Law enforcement when legally appropriate

Personal financial, employment, travel, or relationship information must
only be investigated with appropriate authorization.

PHASE 8 - RECOVERY

After containment:
- Restore trusted configurations.
- Rotate affected credentials.
- Review privileged permissions.
- Validate audit logging.
- Verify endpoint integrity.
- Review DLP policies.
- Confirm monitoring coverage.

PHASE 9 - LESSONS LEARNED

Document:
- Root cause or unresolved hypotheses
- Control failures
- Detection opportunities
- Patient impact
- Data exposure
- Required architectural changes
- Follow-up monitoring

EXIT CRITERIA

Incident response should not close until:
- Evidence is preserved.
- Scope is documented.
- Required notifications are evaluated.
- Access controls are reviewed.
- Patient safety assessment is complete.
- Remediation actions have owners and deadlines.

DESIGN PRINCIPLE

Contain safely, preserve evidence, investigate objectively, protect
patients, and restore trusted operations.

This runbook is for defensive incident response only.
